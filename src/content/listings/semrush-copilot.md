<h2>What Is Semrush Copilot?</h2>
<p>Semrush Copilot is the AI assistant built into the Semrush platform. It reads the data already in your projects, merges findings from six tools into one recommendation list, and ranks that list by priority on the dashboard you see after logging in. According to Semrush, its data sources are Site Audit, Position Tracking, Backlink Analytics, Organic Research, Keyword Gap, and Backlink Gap. Instead of opening each report every morning, you scan one queue and decide what to handle today.</p>
<p>One naming note before anything else. This Copilot has nothing to do with Microsoft's Copilot. Other Semrush docs mention Copilot when describing which external AI assistants the AI Traffic reports track, and that is a different feature from the recommendation engine covered here.</p>
<h2>Core Capabilities and Typical Workflow</h2>
<p>Semrush groups what Copilot does into a few buckets. Daily alerts push Site Audit findings and changes straight to the project dashboard. Prioritization weighs data across tools to suggest what to fix first. Opportunity discovery compares organic rankings and keyword gaps to surface terms worth chasing. Competitor updates flag where rivals are gaining ground. The official blog also markets a set of scenario names such as keyword rescue, backlink detective, broken page fixer, and traffic analyst. Treat those as packaging; under the hood each one is a combination of the same six data sources.</p>
<p>The workflow has three steps. First, set up your project properly and configure Site Audit and Position Tracking, because recommendations degrade quickly when the underlying data is thin. Second, check the Copilot queue each day, either for one project or across all projects. Third, pick the high-priority items, open the underlying report behind each one, and verify before acting. In its February 2025 announcement post on r/SEMrush, Semrush quoted a customer who starts every morning with Copilot instead of manually opening five or six reports, saving roughly an hour a day. That is a vendor-quoted anecdote from a single user, not a benchmark.</p>
<h2>Pricing (Verified September 2026)</h2>
<p>Copilot is included with a Semrush subscription at no extra charge. The subscription itself was restructured in 2026, and the old Pro, Guru, and Business tiers are no longer sold. The current entry plan is called SEO at $139.95/month, or $117.33/month billed annually, covering 5 websites and 500 tracked keywords. Above it sit Starter at $199/month, Pro+ at $299/month, and Advanced at $549/month, with annual billing saving roughly 16 to 17 percent. Every tier ships with a single user seat, and additional seats start at $45/month each. A limited free account exists for a handful of daily lookups. Paid plans show a free trial option, but Semrush does not document one consistent trial length, so check the signup screen before entering a card. Prices move, so treat the <a href="https://www.semrush.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official pricing page</a> as the source of truth.</p>
<h2>Three Practical Tips</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Finish project setup before judging the recommendations.</strong> The quality of the queue depends on crawl settings, tracked keywords, and competitor lists. Run a full Site Audit first, then evaluate what Copilot surfaces.</li>
<li><strong>Treat the queue as triage, not answers.</strong> Open the underlying report for every recommendation before acting. A new user asked in the announcement thread how to fix pages Copilot flagged as broken, which is itself a sign that recommendations usually name the problem without a repair path.</li>
<li><strong>Budget by real seat count.</strong> The entry plan at $139.95 plus one colleague is already $184.95/month, and adding white-label reporting pushes it near $250. Calculate against your actual configuration, not the headline price.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Limitations and Risks</h2>
<p>Copilot executes nothing. Fixing redirects, changing code, and sending outreach emails remain human work. Its coverage is capped by plan quotas, and the entry tier's 5 sites and 500 keywords will constrain an agency quickly. Early versions drew real criticism: an August 2024 r/SEMrush thread complained about low-quality suggestions, and the official reply offered only a way to minimize the panel, with no off switch at the time. Semrush has not published a full changelog of what improved since.</p>
<p>The corporate backdrop changed too. Adobe announced its Semrush acquisition in November 2025 and closed the deal on April 28, 2026, at roughly $1.9 billion. In the community reactions Search Engine Journal collected, voices like Cyrus Shepard worried that an enterprise-focused Adobe could make Semrush less attractive to smaller operators over time. That reading is industry speculation, not an announced plan. Separately, when we checked in September 2026, the dedicated Copilot page in Semrush's knowledge base no longer resolves, so the in-product interface is the current reference.</p>
<h2>Who Should Use It, and Who Shouldn't</h2>
<p>It fits SEO teams and agencies already paying for Semrush, especially anyone managing several client sites who would otherwise hop between reports all day. It holds little value for a solo site owner who only does keyword research and rank tracking, and it is not worth a full platform subscription on its own. It is also not an autonomous fix-it tool or a replacement for monitoring, because every recommendation still needs someone who understands the site to judge it.</p>
