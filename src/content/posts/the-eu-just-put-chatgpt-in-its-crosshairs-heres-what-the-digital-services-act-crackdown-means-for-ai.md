<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On Friday, April 11, the European Commission confirmed it is formally assessing whether OpenAI&#8217;s ChatGPT should be classified as a Very Large Online Platform under the Digital Services Act. The trigger? ChatGPT reported over 120 million monthly active users in the EU — well above the 45 million threshold that brings the heaviest regulatory scrutiny.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Happened</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier stated that &#8220;OpenAI has published user numbers for ChatGPT above the 45 million DSA threshold for designation&#8221; and that the Commission is &#8220;currently assessing this information.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">German newspaper Handelsblatt first reported that ChatGPT would be classified as a very large search engine, which would subject it to stricter compliance, transparency, and risk management requirements under the DSA. This is the same regulatory framework that already applies to platforms like Google, Meta, and X.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why It Matters</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If the Commission formally designates ChatGPT as a VLOP, OpenAI would face obligations including:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Annual risk assessments</strong> of systemic risks related to its AI systems</li>
<li><strong>Independent audits</strong> of its content moderation and recommendation algorithms</li>
<li><strong>Transparency reporting</strong> on how its models surface and rank information</li>
<li><strong>Crisis response protocols</strong> when AI-generated content amplifies disinformation during elections or public health emergencies</li>
<li><strong>Researcher access</strong> to platform data for independent analysis</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Bigger Picture</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is the first time a generative AI product — not a traditional social media or search platform — has been pulled into the DSA&#8217;s strictest regulatory tier. It sets a precedent that could reshape how AI companies operate in Europe.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The move also signals that EU regulators view conversational AI not just as a tool, but as an information intermediary with the same societal reach as search engines and social networks. With 120 million EU users, ChatGPT influences how millions of people access news, research topics, and form opinions — making it, in the Commission&#8217;s view, a platform that requires public accountability.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What OpenAI Said</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">OpenAI responded that it had &#8220;published the average number of monthly active recipients for ChatGPT search in line with existing obligations,&#8221; adding that the figure referred to EU users over the past six months. The company has not yet commented on the potential VLOP designation.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What This Means for the AI Industry</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For AI companies building consumer-facing products, this is a wake-up call. The EU is making clear that scale brings responsibility — regardless of whether your product is a social feed, a search engine, or a chatbot. Expect other AI platforms with significant European user bases to face similar scrutiny in the coming months.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The designation process could take several months. If confirmed, OpenAI would have four months to comply with the full VLOP requirements. Non-compliance carries fines of up to 6% of global revenue.</p>
