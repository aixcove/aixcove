<p>ChatGPT is on the edge of the rulebook it has so far escaped. On July 30, 2026, a European Commission spokesperson confirmed to Reuters that both ChatGPT and Roblox could be included in the list of "very large online platforms" (VLOPs) under the Digital Services Act — the EU's strictest tier of content-moderation obligations, reserved for services with 45 million-plus monthly EU users. Bloomberg, citing anonymous sources, reported the designation is being prepared. By late August, another front opened: EU officials are weighing whether ChatGPT's search functionality makes it a "very large online search engine" as well — a legally thornier question the Table.Briefings policy newsletter called a landmark decision in the making.</p>

<h2>The user numbers forced the issue</h2>
<p>The DSA's VLOP threshold is 45 million average monthly EU users — a bar ChatGPT blew past some time ago. Sam Altman said in October 2025 that ChatGPT had hit 800 million weekly active users worldwide. The Information reported on July 28, 2026 that the service was nearing 1 billion weekly actives, citing internal data, and by July 31 both WSJ and OpenAI's own "Building abundant intelligence" post confirmed the 1 billion user mark. The Verge reported on August 11 that ChatGPT and Gemini had both passed 1 billion users. Even a EU-only fraction of that scale lands far above the threshold, which is why the Commission's review moved from "whether" to "how."</p>

<h2>What VLOP status actually requires</h2>
<p>The DSA's toughest tier is not a fine regime — it is an obligations package. Designated platforms must:</p>
<ul>
<li>run annual independent risk assessments of systemic content risks and audit them externally;</li>
<li>give regulators and vetted researchers API access to platform data;</li>
<li>maintain a complaint system and out-of-court dispute settlement for users;</li>
<li>assess and mitigate risks to minors — the part with teeth for a service heavily used by teenagers;</li>
<li>publish transparency reports on moderation, and in ChatGPT's case, likely on how generated answers can surface problematic content.</li>
</ul>
<p>The current designated list (26 services, per the Commission's page updated July 24, 2026) includes X, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Google Search, Bing, Wikipedia, and Amazon Store. Notably, ChatGPT is not yet on it — the designation is pending, which is exactly what the July reporting was about.</p>

<h2>The open question: is ChatGPT a search engine?</h2>
<p>The more interesting legal puzzle is categorization. ChatGPT answers questions, cites sources, and increasingly functions as a search replacement — but it is generative, non-deterministic, and was not built as an index of the web. The DSA defines VLOSEs (very large online search engines) by function, and EU regulators must decide whether an AI chat interface qualifies. A "yes" would extend a second set of obligations and set precedent for every AI answer engine — Perplexity, Gemini, Grok — operating in the bloc. Table.Briefings framed the August debate as the EU facing a definitional decision with consequences well beyond one product.</p>

<h2>Why it matters for everyone else</h2>
<p>Three takeaways beyond Brussels process-watching:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Brussels is regulating by user count, not by product category.</strong> Once an AI service reaches DSA scale, "it's just a chatbot" stops being a defense. Every consumer AI product with EU ambitions should be building risk-assessment and data-access capacity now.</li>
<li><strong>Minors are the sharpest edge.</strong> Roblox's inclusion in the same review cycle signals where the Commission's attention sits: child safety, addictive design, and exposure to harmful content. ChatGPT's teen usage makes the same lens apply.</li>
<li><strong>The timeline is short.</strong> Once designated, a platform has four months to comply with the full obligations package. If the designation lands this autumn, ChatGPT's compliance sprint starts immediately.</li>
</ul>
