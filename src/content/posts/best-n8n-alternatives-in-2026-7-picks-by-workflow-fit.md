  <h2>为什么人们寻找 n8n 替代品</h2>
  <p>当技术操作员想要控制权时，n8n 最具优势。官方网站强调 500 多种集成、自定义 API 连接、自托管、人机协作 AI 工作流、基于 Git 的版本控制、SSO、LDAP、审计日志和日志流。这是严肃的自动化基础设施。</p>

  <p>定价是人们比较选项的另一个原因。<a href="https://n8n.io/pricing/">n8n 的托管计划</a> 包括共享项目、并发执行限制、工作流历史、AI 积分，以及更高级的自托管企业功能，如 SSO、环境、Git 版本控制和扩展选项。该模式对技术团队来说合理。对于只想实现"当潜在客户表单到达时，总结内容并更新 CRM"的小企业主来说，这可能显得不够透明。</p>
  <p>底线：如果维护、入职或买家信心比最大工作流控制更重要，就应该考虑离开 n8n。</p>
  <h2>1. Zapier：最佳托管 n8n 替代品</h2>
  <p>如果您的团队想要自动化而不关心工作节点在哪里运行，Zapier 是明显的选择。该组中它拥有最广泛的应用覆盖，Zapier 的定价页面描述了 9000 多种应用连接、基于任务的使用方式、付费计划上的 webhooks、共享团队工作流、更高级别的 SAML SSO，以及 Agents、Chatbots 和 MCP 访问等 AI 产品。</p><p>问题是，Zapier 并不是要成为 n8n。它隐藏了内部连接。这对营销运营、销售运营、支持团队和创始人来说很好，他们宁愿今天就推出一个简单的工作流，而不是下周构建一个完美的。如果您需要自托管、复杂的分支逻辑、随处自定义代码或开发者风格的部署流程，Zapier 就不太能满足需求。</p>
  <p>定价从免费计划开始，然后付费自助服务计划根据任务使用量和功能级别进行扩展。这种任务模式是关键点。起初很容易理解，但当工作流有很多步骤或高流量时，成本可能会变得很高。</p>
  <p><strong>选择 Zapier 如果：</strong>您更看重可靠性、应用覆盖率和低培训界面，而不是完全控制权。</p>

  <p><strong>选择Pipedream如果：</strong>您的工作流程大量使用API，并且您希望在不放弃代码的情况下实现更快的自动化。</p>

  <p><strong>选择Activepieces如果：</strong>您想要具有更低摩擦感的产品体验的开源自动化。</p>

  <p>与n8n相比，Power Automate对于想要开源控制权或自定义开发工作流程的构建者来说吸引力较小。当IT治理、身份验证、审批和Microsoft集成比优雅更重要时，它更具优势。</p><p>关键问题是贵公司谁负责自动化。如果是微软租户内的中央IT或业务系统团队，Power Automate应该列入候选名单。如果是连接各种SaaS工具的初创公司运营人员，Make或Zapier通常感觉更快。</p>
  <p><strong>选择Power Automate如果：</strong>贵公司已经使用Microsoft 365作为操作系统。</p>
  <h2>6. Tray.io：最适合企业集成团队</h2>
  <p>Tray.io不是n8n的简单替代品。它更应该被理解为企业自动化和集成平台，适用于关注治理、数据迁移、连接器策略和内部平台标准的团队。</p>
  <p>n8n吸引技术构建者和自动化通才，而Tray则更适合有专门集成负责人的大公司。定价通常由销售主导，这对小团队来说可能过于复杂。但对于试图在营销、销售、支持、财务和数据分析运营中标准化自动化的公司来说，这种企业层可能是关键所在。</p>
  <p><strong>选择Tray.io如果：</strong>您的自动化问题是组织规模，而不仅仅是构建工作流。</p>
  <h2>7. Node-RED：最适合技术自托管自动化</h2>
  <p>Node-RED是这份列表中的特例，这也是它的有用之处。它是一个开源的基于流程的编程工具，已被广泛应用于物联网、硬件、本地系统和自定义集成。它不是试图成为一个完善的SaaS自动化套件。</p>
  <p>如果您因为n8n可以自托管而对其感兴趣，Node-RED可能值得一看，特别是对于以本地为中心或设备密集型自动化。如果您因为n8n可以将业务应用与现代工作流界面连接起来而感兴趣，Node-RED可能会感觉过于技术化和过于简陋。</p>
  <p><strong>选择Node-RED如果：</strong>您正在构建接近设备、本地服务或自定义基础设施的技术自动化。</p>
  <h2>如何选择合适的n8n替代品</h2>
  <p>从所有权开始。如果将由开发人员维护工作流，请测试Pipedream、Activepieces，甚至Node-RED。如果将由运营人员维护它们，请从Make或Zapier开始。如果IT拥有整个技术栈且Microsoft 365无处不在，Power Automate是那个虽然平淡但明智的选择。</p><p>接下来考虑托管问题。自托管控制听起来很棒，直到有人需要修补它、监控它，并向销售团队解释运行失败的情况。如果确实需要控制权，n8n 和 Activepieces 是最接近的选择。如果控制主要是偏好问题，托管工具通常能节省时间。</p>
  <p>最后，要评估真实的流程成本，而不是套餐价格。当每个 AI 步骤、webhook、分支或高容量运行都计入使用量时，便宜的自动化工具可能会变得昂贵。在切换前构建一个代表性的工作流程。包括重试、错误处理和那些棘手的边缘情况。自动化工具的真实价值就在这些地方显现。</p>
  <h2>最终结论</h2>
  <p>对于大多数非技术团队来说，n8n 的最佳替代品是 Zapier。最佳可视化构建器是 Make。最佳面向开发者的选项是 Pipedream。最佳开源替代品是 Activepieces。</p>
  <p>但如果您离开 n8n 的唯一原因是它需要付出努力，请谨慎。一旦真实的业务流程依赖于它，任何认真的自动化堆栈都需要付出努力。更明智的做法是让工具与维护它的人相匹配。比起功能列表，这个人决定了自动化系统能否持续运行。</p>

<p>If n8n is starting to feel like too much platform for the job, the best n8n alternatives in 2026 depend on what you are trying to escape. Zapier is the easy hosted choice, Make is better for visual operations teams, Pipedream suits developers, Activepieces is the closest open-source switch, and Power Automate makes sense if your company already lives in Microsoft 365.</p>
<p>That is the short version. The longer answer is messier, because n8n is not weak. It is one of the more flexible automation platforms around, especially if you care about self-hosting, custom API calls, AI workflow nodes, and keeping more control over how automations run. The problem is fit. Some teams want less infrastructure. Some want clearer pricing. Some want a friendlier builder for non-technical staff. Others want something even more developer-native than n8n.</p>
<p>This guide is for that decision. Not &#8220;which automation platform has the biggest feature list?&#8221; The useful question is: which tool will your team actually maintain six months from now?</p>

<h2>Why People Look for n8n Alternatives</h2>
<p>n8n is strongest when a technical operator wants control. The official site emphasizes 500-plus integrations, custom API connections, self-hosting, human-in-the-loop AI workflows, Git-based version control, SSO, LDAP, audit logs, and log streaming. That is serious automation infrastructure.</p>
<p>But infrastructure has a price, even when the software itself is flexible. Self-hosting means updates, backups, worker scaling, secrets, monitoring, and the occasional workflow that fails at 2 a.m. Hosted n8n removes some of that work, but then you are comparing it against mature SaaS automation tools with bigger app directories and more hand-holding.</p>
<p>Pricing is another reason people compare options. <a href="https://n8n.io/pricing/">n8n&#8217;s hosted plans</a> include shared projects, concurrent execution limits, workflow history, AI credits, and higher-end self-hosted business features such as SSO, environments, Git version control, and scaling options. That model is reasonable for technical teams. It can feel opaque for a small business owner who just wants &#8220;when a lead form arrives, summarize it and update the CRM.&#8221;</p>
<p>Bottom line: switch away from n8n if maintenance, onboarding, or buyer confidence matters more than maximum workflow control.</p>
<h2>1. Zapier: Best Hosted n8n Alternative</h2>
<p>Zapier is the obvious pick if your team wants automation without caring where the workers run. It has the broadest app coverage in this group, with Zapier&#8217;s pricing page describing 9,000-plus app connections, task-based usage, webhooks on paid plans, shared team workflows, SAML SSO at higher tiers, and AI products such as Agents, Chatbots, and MCP access.</p>
<p>The thing is, Zapier is not trying to be n8n. It hides the wiring. That is good for marketing ops, sales ops, support teams, and founders who would rather ship a simple workflow today than build a perfect one next week. It is less satisfying if you want self-hosting, complex branching, custom code everywhere, or a developer-style deployment process.</p>
<p>Pricing starts with a free plan, then paid self-serve plans scale by task usage and feature level. That task model is the catch. It is easy to understand at first, then it can get expensive when workflows have many steps or high volume.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Zapier if:</strong> you want reliability, app coverage, and a low-training interface more than full control.</p>
<h2>2. Make: Best Visual Alternative for Operations Teams</h2>
<p>Make is usually the first tool I would test when someone likes the idea of n8n but wants a more polished visual canvas. Its scenario builder is good for teams that need to inspect branches, routers, filters, and data transformations without reading code.</p>
<p>Compared with n8n, Make feels more SaaS-native. You get a visual workflow builder, app modules, scheduling, error handling, and operation-based pricing. It is comfortable for growth teams, agencies, and internal operations users who need more than Zapier&#8217;s simple linear feel but do not want to run their own automation stack.</p>
<p>The limitation is control. Make can handle advanced scenarios, but it is still a hosted product with its own execution model and pricing rules. If your team wants self-hosted workflows, Git-based deployment, or deep custom node development, n8n remains the more controllable choice.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Make if:</strong> your automations have real branching logic and your users are operations people, not backend engineers.</p>
<h2>3. Pipedream: Best n8n Alternative for Developers</h2>
<p>Pipedream is for teams that think in events, APIs, and code. It sits closer to a developer automation platform than a no-code workflow builder. You can connect SaaS triggers, write custom logic, call APIs, and build serverless-style workflows without setting up a full backend project.</p>
<p>That makes Pipedream a strong n8n alternative when the person maintaining the workflow is already comfortable with JavaScript, HTTP requests, OAuth, payloads, and logs. It is especially useful for product teams, internal platform teams, and developer relations workflows where the automation is half integration glue and half code.</p>
<p>It is not the best choice for a non-technical team that wants to drag app blocks around and never think about runtime behavior. In that case, Zapier or Make will feel less intimidating.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Pipedream if:</strong> your workflows are API-heavy and you want faster automation without giving up code.</p>
<h2>4. Activepieces: Best Open-Source n8n Alternative</h2>
<p>Activepieces is the most interesting open-source n8n alternative for small teams right now. Its pricing page lists a free cloud entry point with 10 free active flows, then usage priced by active flow. It also offers unlimited runs, AI agents, MCP servers, tables, and an MIT-licensed Community Edition for self-hosting.</p>
<p>That combination matters. n8n is powerful, but some teams want an open-source workflow platform that feels lighter and friendlier. Activepieces aims at that space: visual automation, AI-aware features, and a self-hosted community option without asking users to become automation infrastructure specialists on day one.</p>
<p>The tradeoff is maturity. n8n has a larger ecosystem, more battle-tested technical workflows, and deeper visibility among automation builders. Activepieces is the one to test if you want open-source control but think n8n feels heavier than your actual use case.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Activepieces if:</strong> you want open-source automation with a lower-friction product feel.</p>
<h2>5. Microsoft Power Automate: Best for Microsoft 365 Teams</h2>
<p>Power Automate is rarely the coolest answer, but in Microsoft-heavy companies it is often the most practical one. If your workflows revolve around Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Excel, approvals, forms, documents, and internal business processes, Power Automate has an unfair advantage: it is already close to the data and permissions.</p>
<p>Compared with n8n, Power Automate is less appealing for builders who want open-source control or custom developer workflows. It is stronger when IT governance, identity, approvals, and Microsoft integration matter more than elegance.</p>
<p>The key question is who owns automation in your company. If it is a central IT or business systems team inside a Microsoft tenant, Power Automate belongs on the shortlist. If it is a startup operator connecting a patchwork of SaaS tools, Make or Zapier will usually feel faster.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Power Automate if:</strong> Microsoft 365 is already the operating system for your company.</p>
<h2>6. Tray.io: Best for Enterprise Integration Teams</h2>
<p>Tray.io is not a casual n8n replacement. It is better understood as an enterprise automation and integration platform for teams that care about governance, data movement, connector strategy, and internal platform standards.</p>
<p>Where n8n attracts technical builders and automation generalists, Tray tends to fit larger companies with dedicated integration owners. The pricing is usually sales-led, which will be overkill for small teams. But for a company trying to standardize automation across marketing, sales, support, finance, and data operations, that enterprise layer can be the whole point.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Tray.io if:</strong> your automation problem is organizational scale, not just building workflows.</p>
<h2>7. Node-RED: Best for Technical Self-Hosted Automation</h2>
<p>Node-RED is the odd one in this list, and that is why it is useful. It is an open-source, flow-based programming tool that has been used heavily for IoT, hardware, local systems, and custom integrations. It is not trying to be a polished SaaS automation suite.</p>
<p>If n8n appeals to you because it can be self-hosted, Node-RED may be worth a look for local-first or device-heavy automation. If n8n appeals because it connects business apps with a modern workflow UI, Node-RED will probably feel too technical and too bare.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Node-RED if:</strong> you are building technical automations close to devices, local services, or custom infrastructure.</p>
<h2>How to Choose the Right n8n Alternative</h2>
<p>Start with ownership. If a developer will maintain the workflows, test Pipedream, Activepieces, or even Node-RED. If operations staff will maintain them, start with Make or Zapier. If IT owns the stack and Microsoft 365 is everywhere, Power Automate is the boring but sensible candidate.</p>
<p>Then look at hosting. Self-hosted control sounds great until someone has to patch it, monitor it, and explain a failed run to the sales team. If control is genuinely required, n8n and Activepieces are the two closest fits here. If control is mostly a preference, hosted tools will usually save time.</p>
<p>Finally, price the real workflow, not the plan card. A cheap automation tool can become expensive when every AI step, webhook, branch, or high-volume run counts against usage. Build one representative workflow before switching. Include retries, error handling, and the ugly edge cases. That is where automation tools reveal themselves.</p>
<h2>Final Verdict</h2>
<p>The best n8n alternative for most non-technical teams is Zapier. The best visual builder is Make. The best developer-focused option is Pipedream. The best open-source switch is Activepieces.</p>
<p>But if your reason for leaving n8n is only that it takes effort, be careful. Any serious automation stack takes effort once real business processes depend on it. The smarter move is to match the tool to the person who will maintain it. That person, more than the feature list, decides whether the automation survives.</p>
