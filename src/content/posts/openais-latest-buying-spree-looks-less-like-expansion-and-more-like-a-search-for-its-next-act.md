  <h2>OpenAI最新收购潮：与其说是扩张，不如说是寻找下一个增长点</h2>
  <p>过去几周，OpenAI接连收购了两家风格迥异的公司，这一组合比任何新闻稿都更有说服力。4月19日，TechCrunch的<em>Equity</em>播客将这两笔交易解读为OpenAI试图解决比填补产品空白更深层的问题。该公司收购了<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/13/openai-has-bought-ai-personal-finance-startup-hiro/">Hiro Finance</a>，一家成立于2024年的个人理财初创公司，此前还收购了<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/02/openai-acquires-tbpn-the-buzzy-founder-led-business-talk-show/">TBPN</a>，一档在硅谷颇具影响力的创始人主导商业访谈节目。这些动作不像是一家安于现状的公司会做出的。</p>
  <p>最值得关注的不是收购本身，而是它们揭示了OpenAI在2026年4月的处境：规模庞大，资金充裕，但越来越意识到仅靠ChatGPT可能不够。</p>
  <h3>两笔收购，两个痛点</h3>
  <p>Hiro看起来像典型的收购式招聘。创始人Ethan Bloch于4月13日宣布了这笔交易，OpenAI向TechCrunch确认了此事。Hiro表示将于4月20日停止运营，并于5月13日删除服务器上的用户数据。Bloch称Hiro员工将加入OpenAI，LinkedIn上列出了约10名与该初创公司相关的人员。这不是平台整合，而是人才争夺。</p>
  <p>不过，细节很重要。Hiro成立于2024年，在收购前约五个月才推出其AI理财规划工具。用户输入薪资、债务和月度支出，然后测试各种假设情景。Bloch表示，该产品经过训练能准确处理财务计算，并提供了让用户验证输出的方式。OpenAI未披露交易价格，但Bloch的过往履历为这笔交易增添了不少分量。他此前创立了Digit，一家于2021年以超过2亿美元出售给Oportun的数字银行。</p><p>TBPN是一档陌生人匹配节目，而这正是它脱颖而出的原因。OpenAI在四月初收购了这档每日秀。节目在YouTube和X上播出三小时，由前创始人John Coogan和Jordi Hays主持，已成为科技界真正的权力走廊。TechCrunch将其描述为硅谷版的SportsCenter。马克·扎克伯格、萨蒂亚·纳德拉、马克·贝尼奥夫和山姆·奥特曼都曾亮相。据《华尔街日报》报道，该业务今年有望带来超过3000万美元的收入。</p>
  <p>OpenAI表示TBPN将保持编辑独立性。山姆·奥特曼甚至写道：“我不指望他们会对我们手下留情，我确信我会通过偶尔的愚蠢决定来帮助实现这一点。”这句话很幽默。对于一家收购经常报道自身及其竞争对手的媒体平台的公司来说，这也是句方便话。</p>
  <h3>企业竞赛开始让OpenAI感到不适</h3>
  <p>TechCrunch在4月19日的讨论将更大的问题摆在了明面上。肖恩·奥凯恩表示，Hiro交易表明OpenAI正在寻找“某种比聊天机器人更有吸引力、或许更值得付费的东西”。这触及了问题的核心。ChatGPT规模庞大，但庞大的使用量和持久的商业实力并非一回事。</p>
  <p>OpenAI仍是AI领域最知名的名字，但当前最关键的市场部分——企业和编码——已不再显得稳固。克尔斯滕·科罗塞克表示，Anthropic“在企业端取得了很大成功”。安东尼·哈进一步称Anthropic是直接竞争对手，而非配角。这与TechCrunch在旧金山HumanX大会上报道的氛围一致，供应商们不断谈论Claude，而ChatGPT被提及的频率要低得多。</p>
  <p>HumanX的报道中有一句话OpenAI可能不愿看到：一位供应商称ChatGPT“走下坡路了”。这个判断是否公平，不如它正在流传这一事实重要。在企业软件领域，认知转化为采购政策的速度比消费应用更快。一旦开发者和内部工具团队认定竞争对手的模型更适合编码工作，这种转变就不是表面功夫。它会变成预算、工作流程和习惯。</p>
  <h3>OpenAI正同时应对产品风险和形象风险</h3><p>这两笔交易与OpenAI眼下的麻烦几乎严丝合缝。Hiro对应产品风险，TBPN对应形象风险。但两剂药方都不保证见效。</p>
  <p>产品端的财务逻辑一目了然。OpenAI早已把ChatGPT定位成对财务团队有用的工具。收购一支做过带数学验证的消费级财务助手的团队，可能帮助公司围绕规划、情景建模或专用智能体搭建更紧密的工作流。如果OpenAI想让客户为结果付费，而不是只为打开一个聊天窗口付费，这一步就有分量。</p>
  <p>形象端的情况更复杂。TBPN将向Chris Lehane汇报，他是公司里政治经验最丰富的高管之一。TechCrunch提到，Lehane于2024年加入OpenAI，在政策和公共定位上发力很猛。Fidji Simo称赞TBPN“出色的传播和营销直觉”，说这档节目能帮助“以让大众理解这项技术对日常生活全部影响的方式，把AI带给世界”。这话听着圆熟，但也说明OpenAI知道自己有叙事问题。</p>
  <p>作者的判断很直接：一家公司在同一段时间里买下金融初创公司和科技脱口秀，是在寻找新的施力点，因为核心故事已经很难单靠势头来辩护。OpenAI仍有规模，但也有审视、更强的对手，以及一个现在期待实用智能体、而不是第一次演示日后就褪色的炫目demo的市场。</p>
  <h3>接下来意味着什么</h3>
  <p>这两笔收购在周末引起这么大动静，是有原因的。据TechCrunch的HumanX报道，OpenAI据称完成了1220亿美元融资，却仍面临专注度质疑。公司最近推出100美元的ChatGPT层级，附带更多Codex权限，这又是一个信号：编程和企业收入现在是核心，不是可选项。</p>
  <p>这就是为什么4月19日的播客对话比任何一笔收购标题本身都重要。这些交易暗示，OpenAI正试图同时回答两个棘手问题：聊天机器人之后是什么？当Anthropic等对手不断赢得开发者尊重时，公司如何掌控叙事？</p><p>Hiro或许能回答第一个问题，TBPN或许能回答第二个。但这两笔收购都改变不了一个更大的事实：AI竞赛已经进入对现有巨头不太有利的阶段。品牌本身作为护城河的作用正在减弱。买家要的是能节省时间、能写代码、能在生产环境中站得住脚的产品。OpenAI仍然能赢下这场竞争，只是它看起来不再能靠默认优势取胜了。</p>

<h2>OpenAI’s Latest Buying Spree Looks Less Like Expansion and More Like a Search for Its Next Act</h2>
<p>OpenAI spent the past few weeks buying two very different companies, and that pairing says more than any press release could. On April 19, TechCrunch’s <em>Equity</em> podcast framed the deals as a sign that OpenAI is trying to solve deeper problems than product gap-filling. The company picked up <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/13/openai-has-bought-ai-personal-finance-startup-hiro/">Hiro Finance</a>, a personal finance startup founded in 2024, and earlier bought <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/02/openai-acquires-tbpn-the-buzzy-founder-led-business-talk-show/">TBPN</a>, a founder-led business talk show with serious reach in Silicon Valley. Those are not the moves of a company that feels settled.</p>
<p>The most interesting part is not the acquisitions themselves. It is what they reveal about OpenAI’s position in April 2026: huge scale, huge funding, and a growing sense that ChatGPT alone may not be enough.</p>
<h3>Two purchases, two pressure points</h3>
<p>Hiro looks like a classic acqui-hire. Founder Ethan Bloch announced the deal on April 13, and OpenAI confirmed it to TechCrunch. Hiro said it would shut down operations on April 20 and delete user data from its servers on May 13. Bloch said Hiro employees would join OpenAI, and LinkedIn listed about 10 people associated with the startup. That is not a platform roll-up. It is a talent grab.</p>
<p>Still, the details matter. Hiro was only founded in 2024 and launched its AI financial planning tool about five months before the acquisition. Users fed in salary, debt, and monthly spending, then tested what-if scenarios. Bloch said the product was trained to handle financial math accurately and included a way for users to verify outputs. OpenAI did not disclose the price, but Bloch’s track record gives the move some weight. He previously founded Digit, a neobank sold to Oportun in 2021 for more than $200 million.</p>
<p>TBPN is a stranger fit, and that is exactly why it stands out. OpenAI acquired the daily show in early April. The program runs for three hours on YouTube and X, is hosted by former founders John Coogan and Jordi Hays, and has become a real power corridor for the tech world. TechCrunch described it as a kind of SportsCenter for Silicon Valley. Mark Zuckerberg, Satya Nadella, Marc Benioff, and Sam Altman have all appeared on it. According to <em>The Wall Street Journal</em>, the business is on track to pull in more than $30 million this year.</p>
<p>OpenAI said TBPN would keep editorial independence. Sam Altman even wrote, “I don’t expect them to go any easier on us, am sure I’ll do my part to help enable that with occasional stupid decisions.” That line is funny. It is also a convenient line for a company buying a media platform that often covers the company and its rivals.</p>
<h3>The enterprise race is starting to look uncomfortable for OpenAI</h3>
<p>TechCrunch’s April 19 discussion put the larger issue in plain view. Sean O’Kane said the Hiro deal suggests OpenAI is searching for “something else that may have more hooks than just a chatbot, and maybe something worth paying more for.” That gets to the center of the problem. ChatGPT is enormous, but enormous usage and durable business strength are not the same thing.</p>
<p>OpenAI is still the most recognizable name in AI, yet a chunk of the market that matters most right now, enterprise and coding, no longer looks locked up. Kirsten Korosec said Anthropic is “having a lot of success on the enterprise side of things.” Anthony Ha went further and called Anthropic a direct competitor, not a side character. That matches the mood TechCrunch reported from the HumanX conference in San Francisco, where vendors kept talking about Claude while ChatGPT came up far less often.</p>
<p>That HumanX reporting carried a line OpenAI should probably hate: one vendor said ChatGPT had “gone downhill.” Whether that verdict is fair matters less than the fact that it is circulating. In enterprise software, perception can turn into procurement policy faster than consumer apps do. Once developers and internal tool teams decide a rival model is better for coding work, the switch is not cosmetic. It becomes budget, workflow, and habit.</p>
<h3>OpenAI is trying to fix product risk and image risk at the same time</h3>
<p>The two deals line up almost too neatly with OpenAI’s current headaches. Hiro addresses product risk. TBPN addresses image risk. Neither fix is guaranteed.</p>
<p>On the product side, the financial logic is easy to read. OpenAI has already positioned ChatGPT as useful for finance teams. Buying a team that built a consumer finance assistant with math verification could help the company build tighter workflows around planning, scenario modeling, or specialized agents. That would matter if OpenAI wants customers to pay for outcomes instead of just paying for access to a chat window.</p>
<p>On the image side, things are messier. TBPN will report to Chris Lehane, one of the company’s most politically seasoned operators. TechCrunch noted that Lehane joined OpenAI in 2024 and has pushed hard on policy and public positioning. Fidji Simo praised TBPN’s “amazing comms and marketing instincts” and said the show could help “bring AI to the world in a way that helps people understand the full impact of this technology on their daily lives.” That sounds polished. It also sounds like OpenAI knows it has a narrative problem.</p>
<p>The author’s read is blunt: when a company buys a finance startup and a tech talk show in the same stretch, it is searching for new pressure points because the core story has become harder to defend on momentum alone. OpenAI still has scale. It also has scrutiny, stronger rivals, and a market that now expects useful agents instead of dazzling demos that fade after the first demo day.</p>
<h3>What this means next</h3>
<p>There is a reason these acquisitions landed so loudly over the weekend. OpenAI raised a reported $122 billion funding round, according to TechCrunch’s HumanX coverage, and still faces questions about focus. The company recently pushed a $100 ChatGPT tier with more Codex access, another sign that coding and enterprise revenue are now central, not optional.</p>
<p>That is why the April 19 podcast conversation matters more than either acquisition headline by itself. The deals hint that OpenAI is trying to answer two uncomfortable questions at once. What comes after the chatbot? And how does the company control the story when competitors such as Anthropic keep winning respect with developers?</p>
<p>Hiro may help with the first question. TBPN may help with the second. But neither purchase changes the bigger fact that the AI race has entered a less flattering phase for incumbents. Brand alone is fading as a moat. Buyers want products that save time, write code, and hold up in production. OpenAI can still win that fight. It just no longer looks like the company gets to win it by default.</p>
