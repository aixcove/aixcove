  <h2>Dify vs Flowise：快速结论</h2>
  <p><strong>选Dify，如果</strong>你需要内置的知识库管理、更强的团队功能、更干净的应用部署，以及一个更接近AI产品工作区而非画布工具的平台。</p>
  <p><strong>选Flowise，如果</strong>你想要快速自托管起步、更贴合开发者习惯的可视化构建器、对LangChain风格组件更熟悉，以及托管计划上更低的前期平台成本。</p>
  <p>问题是，这两个工具在表面上有很多重叠。两者都能构建AI代理、RAG应用和聊天工作流。两者都支持自托管。两者都能连接多个模型提供商。但一旦你越过落地页的相似之处，它们瞄准的是不同的操作风格。</p>
  <h2>Dify做得更好的地方</h2>
  <h3>1. 内置的产品结构</h3>
  <p>Dify不只是一个流程编辑器。它把提示词管理、应用部署、知识库、模型连接、日志和工作区控制打包成一个产品。当你的项目越过演示阶段时，这一点很关键。</p><p>与其把构建器、检索层和部署模式拼凑在一起，Dify 给你的是一个更明确的整套方案。对很多团队来说，这是优点，不是限制。</p>
  <h3>2. 更适合 RAG 密集型应用</h3>
  <p>Dify 在检索流程上投入了大量精力。它的平台包含文档处理、知识库管理、分块控制、检索设置，以及对更高级搜索模式的支持。如果你的主要场景是“跟公司文档对话”或“在杂乱文件之上构建内部助手”，Dify 通常能让你少做手工组装。</p>
  <p>Flowise 完全能做 RAG，只是它更偏向组件层面。你需要自己连接加载器、向量库、检索器、重排序器和模型节点。想要控制力，这很好；但团队想要开箱即用的更干净运营层，就没那么好了。</p>
  <h3>3. 团队工作流和管理控制</h3>
  <p>Dify 明显更适合多人使用。它的付费方案包含多个团队成员、工作区结构、应用限制，以及面向内部团队或客户部署的运营控制。</p>
  <p>Flowise 也有团队和工作区，尤其在更高方案里，但它的重心仍然更偏向构建者，而不是工作区。</p>
  <h2>Flowise 做得更好的地方</h2>
  <h3>1. 对技术型构建者上手更快</h3>
  <p>如果你习惯用节点思考，Flowise 更容易让人喜欢。它的可视化界面很直接：连接组件、路由逻辑、测试输出、重复。如果你已经理解链、工具、检索器和代理步骤怎么组合，Flowise 往往比 Dify 更顺手。</p>
  <p>这就是为什么开发者一直推荐它做原型、实验和自定义内部工具。</p>
  <h3>2. 自托管占用更轻</h3>
  <p>这是最实际的差异之一。Dify 通常跑起来是一套更重的多服务架构。Flowise 更轻，上线更简单，尤其适合用普通 VPS 的独立开发者或小团队。基础设施预算紧张的话，Flowise 还没开始构建就有优势。</p>
  <p>一句话：如果你想这周就在便宜的基础设施上试个想法，Flowise 通常摩擦更少。</p>
  <h3>3. 对 LangChain 导向的工作流吸引力更强</h3><p>Flowise与LangChain生态绑定很深，这一点在使用中会明显感受到。如果你想对接大量模型供应商、向量库、自定义工具，或者使用与LangChain概念对应的逻辑模式，Flowise很合适。</p>
  <p>Dify也支持广泛的模型栈，但它的用户体验更产品化。Flowise更像一个可视化开发者框架。</p>

  <p>如果你的用户是市场人员、运营团队，或者只需要一个能用的助手而非灵活流程图表的内部干系人，Dify更稳妥。</p>
  <p>如果你的用户是开发者、解决方案架构师，或者技术能力强的运营人员，Flowise往往更快，因为它不碍事。</p>

<p>If you are choosing between Dify and Flowise in 2026, the short answer is this: <strong>Dify is usually the better pick for teams building production AI apps with RAG, multiple users, and cleaner operational controls</strong>. <strong>Flowise is the better pick for developers who want a lighter, more flexible, more visual builder they can self-host fast</strong>.</p>
<p>That is the real split. Dify feels like a full AI application platform. Flowise feels like a developer-friendly workflow canvas that gives you more freedom, but also more responsibility.</p>
<p>Pricing matters early here. Dify Cloud starts at <strong>$59 per workspace per month</strong> for the Professional plan, with a free sandbox tier. Flowise Cloud starts at <strong>$35 per month</strong> for Starter, plus a free tier with limits. Both tools can be self-hosted, but Flowise is generally the easier and lighter setup, while Dify gives you more built-in product structure once it is running.</p>
<p>If you already know your team is going to build customer-facing assistants, internal knowledge apps, or multi-step AI workflows with non-technical stakeholders involved, I would lean Dify. If you are prototyping fast, prefer node-based editing, or want tighter control through the LangChain ecosystem, I would lean Flowise.</p>
<h2>Dify vs Flowise: quick verdict</h2>
<p><strong>Choose Dify if</strong> you need built-in knowledge base management, stronger team features, cleaner app deployment, and a platform that feels closer to an AI product workspace than a canvas tool.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Flowise if</strong> you want a fast self-hosted start, a more developer-shaped visual builder, deeper comfort with LangChain-style components, and lower upfront platform cost on the hosted plan.</p>
<p>The thing is, these tools overlap on the surface. Both let you build AI agents, RAG apps, and chat workflows. Both support self-hosting. Both can connect to multiple model providers. But once you get past the landing-page similarities, they are aimed at different operating styles.</p>
<h2>What Dify does better</h2>
<h3>1. Built-in product structure</h3>
<p>Dify is not just a flow editor. It bundles prompt management, app deployment, knowledge bases, model connections, logs, and workspace controls into one product. That matters when your project moves past a demo.</p>
<p>Instead of stitching together a builder, a retrieval layer, and a deployment pattern, Dify gives you a more opinionated stack. For a lot of teams, that is a feature, not a limitation.</p>
<h3>2. Better fit for RAG-heavy apps</h3>
<p>Dify puts a lot of attention into retrieval workflows. Its platform includes document handling, knowledge base management, chunking controls, retrieval settings, and support for more advanced search patterns. If your main use case is “chat with company docs” or “build an internal assistant on top of messy files,” Dify usually gets you there with less manual assembly.</p>
<p>Flowise can absolutely do RAG. It just tends to feel more component-level. You connect loaders, vector stores, retrievers, rerankers, and model nodes yourself. That is great if you want control. It is less great if your team wants a cleaner operational layer out of the box.</p>
<h3>3. Team workflows and admin controls</h3>
<p>Dify is more obviously built for shared use. Its paid plans include multiple team members, workspace structure, app limits, and operational controls that make sense for internal teams or client-facing deployments.</p>
<p>Flowise has teams and workspaces too, especially on higher plans, but its center of gravity still feels more builder-first than workspace-first.</p>
<h2>What Flowise does better</h2>
<h3>1. Faster path for technical builders</h3>
<p>Flowise is easier to like if you think in nodes. Its visual interface is direct: connect components, route logic, test outputs, repeat. If you already understand how chains, tools, retrievers, and agent steps fit together, Flowise often feels more natural than Dify.</p>
<p>That is why developers keep recommending it for prototypes, experiments, and custom internal tooling.</p>
<h3>2. Lighter self-hosting footprint</h3>
<p>This is one of the most practical differences. Dify typically runs as a heavier multi-service stack. Flowise is lighter and simpler to get online, especially for solo builders or small teams using a modest VPS. If infrastructure budget is tight, Flowise has an edge before you even start building.</p>
<p>Bottom line: if you want to test an idea on cheap infrastructure this week, Flowise is usually less friction.</p>
<h3>3. Stronger appeal for LangChain-oriented workflows</h3>
<p>Flowise is closely tied to the LangChain ecosystem, and that shows. It is a good fit if you want to work with a wide set of model providers, vector stores, custom tools, or logic patterns that map well to LangChain concepts.</p>
<p>Dify supports a broad model stack too, but its UX is more productized. Flowise feels closer to a visual developer framework.</p>
<h2>Pricing comparison in 2026</h2>
<p>Here is the practical pricing view based on the official plan pages:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dify</strong>: free Sandbox tier, <strong>$59/month</strong> Professional, <strong>$159/month</strong> Team on cloud.</li>
<li><strong>Flowise</strong>: free tier, <strong>$35/month</strong> Starter, <strong>$65/month</strong> Pro on cloud.</li>
</ul>
<p>That makes Flowise cheaper to start on hosted plans. But cost is not just subscription price.</p>
<p>Dify may save time if your team would otherwise spend hours rebuilding common app structure, managing document workflows, or setting up a more polished shared environment. Flowise may save money if you are self-hosting, staying lean, and do not need Dify’s extra product layer.</p>
<p>So the better value depends on where your bottleneck lives: software spend, setup time, or team coordination.</p>
<h2>Which one is easier to use?</h2>
<p>For non-technical teams, Dify is usually easier to live with.</p>
<p>That sounds backward at first because Flowise is very visual. But visual does not always mean simpler. Flowise exposes more of the moving parts. Dify hides more of them behind a cleaner application model.</p>
<p>If your users are marketers, ops teams, or internal stakeholders who need a working assistant more than a flexible graph, Dify is the safer bet.</p>
<p>If your users are developers, solution architects, or technically comfortable operators, Flowise is often faster because it gets out of the way.</p>
<h2>Best use cases for Dify</h2>
<ul>
<li>Internal knowledge base assistants</li>
<li>Customer support copilots</li>
<li>Multi-user AI apps with permissions and sharing</li>
<li>Teams that want cloud now and self-hosting later</li>
<li>RAG-first applications with less manual assembly</li>
</ul>
<h2>Best use cases for Flowise</h2>
<ul>
<li>Fast prototypes and MVPs</li>
<li>Developer-led agent experiments</li>
<li>Custom workflows built around LangChain-style primitives</li>
<li>Budget-sensitive self-hosted deployments</li>
<li>Teams that want direct control over node logic and integrations</li>
</ul>
<h2>Limitations you should know before choosing</h2>
<p>Dify’s main limitation is that it can feel heavier than you want for small experiments. More features, more structure, more setup. That is great once you need it. It is annoying when you do not.</p>
<p>Flowise has the opposite problem. It is flexible, but you own more of the complexity. Operational polish, governance, and production discipline do not magically appear just because the flow works on your laptop.</p>
<p>One more point that buyers miss: do not assume you can easily migrate later. These tools use different workflow models and UX assumptions. If you switch after building deeply in one system, expect rebuild work.</p>
<h2>So which should you choose?</h2>
<p><strong>Pick Dify</strong> if you are building something that needs to look and behave like a real product, especially with knowledge bases, shared workspaces, and less tolerance for rough edges.</p>
<p><strong>Pick Flowise</strong> if you want speed, lower hosting friction, and a builder that feels closer to a visual dev environment than a managed AI app platform.</p>
<p>If I had to reduce it to one line: <strong>Dify is better for organized teams shipping AI apps; Flowise is better for hands-on builders shaping AI workflows</strong>.</p>
