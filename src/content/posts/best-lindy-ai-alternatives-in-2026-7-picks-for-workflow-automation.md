<p>If Lindy feels close to what you need but not quite right, start by separating two questions: do you want an AI assistant for inbox and calendar work, or do you want a workflow automation platform that can run across your whole business stack? Lindy is strongest for the first job. The best Lindy AI alternatives in 2026 are better when you need lower costs, self-hosting, heavier browser automation, sales workflows, or more control over how agents run.</p>
<p>The pricing piece matters early. Lindy’s current public plans start at $49.99 per month for Plus, then $99.99 for Pro and $199.99 for Max, with higher usage allowances as you move up. That is reasonable if you are replacing assistant work around email, meetings, follow-ups, and light operations. It gets harder to justify when your use case is mostly high-volume automations, GTM scraping, support routing, or internal workflows that fire all day.</p>
<p>Bottom line: if your main need is a polished AI executive assistant, Lindy should stay on the shortlist. If your main need is flexible automation, cheaper execution, local control, or a platform your team can build on, the alternatives below are the ones to compare first.</p>
<h2>Quick Shortlist: Best Lindy AI Alternatives</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Relay.app</strong>: best Lindy alternative for human-in-the-loop team workflows.</li>
<li><strong>Gumloop</strong>: best for no-code AI workflows with a builder-friendly feel.</li>
<li><strong>n8n</strong>: best for self-hosted AI automation and technical operators.</li>
<li><strong>Zapier</strong>: best for broad app coverage and simple business automations.</li>
<li><strong>Make</strong>: best for visual automation at a lower starting cost than many task-based tools.</li>
<li><strong>Bardeen</strong>: best for browser-based research, scraping, and GTM workflows.</li>
<li><strong>Relevance AI</strong>: best for teams building structured AI agents around business processes.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Why People Look for Lindy Alternatives</h2>
<p>Lindy sits in an interesting middle ground. It is easier than building agents from scratch, but more agent-like than a classic automation tool. That is useful for founders, consultants, operators, and small teams who want help with email, meeting prep, scheduling, CRM updates, support replies, and repetitive admin work.</p>
<p>The catch is that not every automation problem wants an assistant-shaped product. Some teams need predictable execution pricing. Some want to self-host workflows. Some need browser actions, scraping, enrichment, or custom API logic. Others already have a mature automation stack and only need AI as one step inside a larger process.</p>
<p>That is where Lindy can feel too narrow. It is not because the product is weak. It is because “AI assistant” and “automation infrastructure” are different buying decisions.</p>
<h2>1. Relay.app: Best for Human-in-the-Loop AI Workflows</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.relay.app/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Relay.app</a> is the first Lindy alternative I would test if your workflows need approval points, clean handoffs, and predictable team operations. It is built around workflow automation with AI agents inside the process, not as a standalone assistant personality.</p>
<p>The big difference is control. Relay is useful when a workflow should pause for a person, route a decision, ask for confirmation, or keep a human in the loop before touching customer-facing systems. That makes it a better fit for operations teams, agencies, recruiters, and customer-facing teams that want automation without handing the whole process to an agent.</p>
<p>Pricing is also easier to test. Relay’s public materials describe a free tier and paid plans that scale by steps and AI credits. Check the current pricing page before budgeting, but the shape is clear enough: you are paying for workflow volume rather than a personal-assistant bundle.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Relay.app over Lindy</strong> if your team cares about approvals, workflow reliability, and repeatable processes across apps. Stick with Lindy if most of the value is personal inbox and meeting assistance.</p>
<h2>2. Gumloop: Best No-Code AI Workflow Builder</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.gumloop.com/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Gumloop</a> is one of the more direct Lindy competitors for people who want AI-powered workflows without writing code. It has a visual builder, agents, flows, triggers, and a credit model that starts with a free plan. Its pricing page currently lists 5,000 credits per month on the free plan and paid Pro pricing from $37 per month.</p>
<p>Where Lindy feels like an assistant you configure, Gumloop feels more like a workspace for building automations. That makes it better for repeatable workflows such as lead enrichment, document processing, research tasks, content operations, support triage, and sales handoffs.</p>
<p>The tradeoff is that you still need to design the workflow. Gumloop lowers the barrier, but it does not remove the need to think through triggers, data inputs, outputs, and failure cases. If you want the product to behave like a ready-made executive assistant, Lindy is simpler. If you want to build custom AI automations with more flexibility, Gumloop is the stronger test.</p>
<h2>3. n8n: Best Self-Hosted Lindy Alternative</h2>
<p><a href="https://n8n.io/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">n8n</a> is the practical pick when control matters more than polish. It is a workflow automation platform with cloud plans and a self-hosted route, which makes it attractive for technical teams that want to own their data path, connect unusual APIs, and put AI inside broader business workflows.</p>
<p>This is not the easiest Lindy replacement. It is the most controllable one. n8n works best when you have someone comfortable with workflows, webhooks, credentials, error handling, and the occasional bit of JavaScript. In return, you get a system that can connect CRMs, databases, Slack, email, internal APIs, model providers, and local AI tools.</p>
<p>Aixcove has already covered this branch in more detail in the <a href="/best-n8n-alternatives-in-2026-7-picks-for-ai-workflows-and-self-hosting/">n8n alternatives guide</a> and the <a href="/how-to-use-n8n-with-ollama-for-private-ai-automation-in-2026/">n8n with Ollama tutorial</a>. The short version: n8n is not trying to be your AI assistant. It is trying to be the automation layer your AI assistant might plug into.</p>
<p><strong>Choose n8n over Lindy</strong> if you need self-hosting, complex workflow logic, or private AI automation. Avoid it if your team wants a friendly assistant UI and minimal setup.</p>
<h2>4. Zapier: Best for App Coverage and Simple Automations</h2>
<p><a href="https://zapier.com/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Zapier</a> is not the most exciting Lindy alternative, but it is still the default for teams that care about app coverage. Zapier says it connects with more than 9,000 apps, and that breadth matters when the job is “move data between the tools we already use.”</p>
<p>Zapier now has AI features across its automation stack, but its core appeal is still reliability and familiarity. Non-technical teams can build Zaps quickly, and the template ecosystem is large enough that common workflows rarely start from a blank page.</p>
<p>The problem is cost at volume. Zapier’s pricing is task-based, and AI steps can consume tasks differently depending on model tier. That is fine for low-volume workflows. It can become expensive once a multi-step automation runs hundreds or thousands of times per month.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Zapier over Lindy</strong> if you need broad app support and simple business automations. Skip it if you are leaving Lindy because usage-based pricing already makes you nervous.</p>
<h2>5. Make: Best Visual Automation Alternative for Operators</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.make.com/en/pricing" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Make</a> is a strong fit for operators who want more visual control than Zapier without jumping all the way to n8n. Its scenarios make branching, routers, and multi-step flows easier to inspect, especially when the workflow has several moving parts.</p>
<p>Make’s pricing is credit-based. The official pricing page explains that module actions in a scenario count as credits, so the real cost depends on how many modules run each time the workflow fires. That is the number to watch. A workflow that looks simple on the canvas can burn more credits than expected if it polls often, loops through many records, or uses several modules per run.</p>
<p>Compared with Lindy, Make is less assistant-like and more automation-native. It is a better choice for scheduled workflows, data syncing, operations dashboards, lead routing, and multi-app processes where AI is only part of the job.</p>
<h2>6. Bardeen: Best for Browser Automation and GTM Research</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.bardeen.ai/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Bardeen</a> makes sense when the work happens in the browser. Think LinkedIn research, web scraping, enrichment, CRM updates, prospect lists, repetitive copy-paste tasks, and sales operations workflows that depend on pages rather than clean APIs.</p>
<p>This is where Lindy and Bardeen split sharply. Lindy is better for assistant workflows around inboxes, meetings, and connected SaaS tools. Bardeen is better when the bottleneck is browser work. Its pricing is credit-based, with public plans that include free credits and paid tiers for heavier scraping and enrichment.</p>
<p>The limitation is scope. Bardeen can be excellent for GTM teams, but it is not the broadest choice for backend workflow orchestration or self-hosted AI automation. Use it when browser-based work is the pain. Do not force it into jobs where n8n, Make, or Relay would be cleaner.</p>
<h2>7. Relevance AI: Best for Structured AI Agent Teams</h2>
<p><a href="https://relevanceai.com/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Relevance AI</a> is worth considering if you are moving from “one AI assistant” to “a set of agents that handle specific business jobs.” Its positioning is more enterprise and agent-workforce oriented, with agents for sales, research, operations, and internal workflows.</p>
<p>The pricing model is more complex than a simple subscription. Relevance AI’s documentation says it split pricing into Actions, which cover what agents do, and Vendor Credits, which cover AI model costs. It also lets teams bring their own API keys in some cases. That can be attractive if your team wants clearer cost accounting around agent work, but it also means you need to understand the billing model before you roll it out broadly.</p>
<p><strong>Choose Relevance AI over Lindy</strong> if you are building structured business agents for a team. Lindy is easier for assistant-style productivity. Relevance AI is more interesting when the agent itself becomes part of your operating model.</p>
<h2>How to Choose the Right Lindy Alternative</h2>
<p>The fastest way to choose is to name the work you are replacing.</p>
<p>If the work is mostly email, calendar, meeting notes, follow-ups, and personal productivity, Lindy is still a good fit. Compare it with Relay only if approvals and team workflows matter more than assistant convenience.</p>
<p>If the work is workflow automation across apps, test Relay, Make, Zapier, and n8n. Relay is the most comfortable for human-in-the-loop flows. Zapier wins on app coverage. Make gives operators more visual control. n8n is the pick when self-hosting and flexibility matter.</p>
<p>If the work is GTM research, browser tasks, scraping, or enrichment, start with Bardeen and Gumloop. If the work is structured agent building for a team, look at Relevance AI and compare it against more technical stacks before committing.</p>
<h2>Final Verdict</h2>
<p>The best Lindy AI alternative for most teams is Relay.app if they want safer team workflows, Gumloop if they want a no-code AI automation builder, and n8n if they want self-hosted control. Zapier and Make are better when the job is classic app-to-app automation. Bardeen is the specialist for browser and GTM work. Relevance AI is the bigger step up when agents become a team-level system rather than a personal assistant.</p>
<p>The real decision is not “which tool has more AI?” It is where the automation needs to live. If it lives in your inbox and calendar, Lindy still makes sense. If it lives across your stack, one of these alternatives will probably fit better.</p>
