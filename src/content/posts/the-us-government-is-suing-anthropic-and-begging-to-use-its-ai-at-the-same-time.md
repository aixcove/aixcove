  <h2>美国政府一边起诉Anthropic，一边求着用它的AI</h2>
  <p>2026年4月14日，Anthropic联合创始人Jack Clark走上Semafor世界经济峰会的舞台，确认了一件听起来像笔误的事：他的公司正在积极向特朗普政府汇报新模型Mythos的情况，尽管五角大楼已将Anthropic列为供应链风险企业，并切断了所有商业往来。</p>
  <p>这不是比喻。两件事同时发生。</p>
  <p>Mythos于4月7日发布，是Anthropic在编程和智能体任务上能力最强的模型。这是公司自己的说法。该模型未对公众开放。Anthropic表示限制访问是因为Mythos虽然未针对网络安全工作训练，却碰巧在发现软件漏洞方面表现出色。这是真谨慎还是聪明的企业销售策略，取决于你问谁。</p>
  <h2>实际发生了什么</h2>
  <p>Mythos发布前，副总统JD Vance和财政部长Scott Bessent召集科技公司CEO讨论AI安全和网络威胁。那次会议由CNBC于4月10日报道，为后续事件埋下伏笔。</p>
  <p>发布后，Bessent和美联储主席Jerome Powell又召集银行高管开会。这次传递的信息不同。据彭博社报道，官员们鼓励这些银行测试Mythos用于漏洞检测。摩根大通已被列为早期合作伙伴。高盛、花旗集团、美国银行和摩根士丹利据报也在测试中。</p>
  <p>美国五家最大的银行，在监管机构私下敦促下，采用一家正被政府告上法庭的公司的模型。</p>
  <h2>法庭之争</h2>
  <p>Anthropic于2026年3月起诉国防部，此前五角大楼将该公司列为供应链风险企业。这一认定源于军方获取Anthropic AI系统的谈判破裂。Anthropic希望限制其模型的使用方式，特别是围绕大规模监控美国人和全自主武器。五角大楼则要求无限制访问。OpenAI最终赢得了那份合同。</p><p>旧金山一名联邦法官后来批准了Anthropic的初步禁令，阻止特朗普政府执行对Claude的封禁。但4月8日，一家上诉法院让Anthropic受挫，拒绝暂时阻止五角大楼的列入黑名单决定。</p>
  <p>克拉克在Semafor活动上淡化了这一争议，称其为“狭隘的合同纠纷”，并表示Anthropic不希望此事干扰国家安全合作。他的原话是：“我们的立场是政府必须了解这些东西，我们必须找到新方式，让政府与正在制造真正革新经济的产品的私营部门合作。”</p>
  <h2>英国监管机构为何关注</h2>
  <p>《金融时报》报道称，英国金融监管机构也在讨论Mythos带来的风险。该模型大规模发现零日漏洞的能力让各国政府紧张，不只是华盛顿。一个能比任何人类团队更快审计代码漏洞的工具，要么是银行梦寐以求的最佳防御武器，要么是落入坏人之手的严重攻击能力。</p>
  <p>《华盛顿邮报》专栏作家4月12日写道，鉴于Mythos检测关键软件缺陷的能力，五角大楼对Anthropic的禁令看起来“短视”。标题几乎不言自明。</p>
  <h2>没人能回答的就业问题</h2>
  <p>克拉克还在峰会上谈到了AI对就业的影响。Anthropic首席执行官Dario Amodei此前警告，AI进步可能将失业率推至大萧条时期水平。克拉克领导Anthropic的经济学家团队，他的表态更为谨慎。他说，公司目前看到“部分行业早期毕业生就业出现一些潜在疲软”。还不是大规模失业，但已有早期震感。</p>
  <p>这值得关注。当制造这项技术的公司承认入门级白领岗位开始走软，这大概不是营销话术。</p>
  <h2>奇怪之处在哪</h2><p>整个局面充满矛盾，这本身就说明了2026年AI政策实际处于什么状态。国防部认为Anthropic风险太高，不愿与其做生意。财政部和美联储则在推动银行采用Anthropic的产品。上诉法院裁定五角大楼可以将Anthropic列入黑名单。下级法院却判政府不能封禁Claude。这家公司一边起诉政府，一边又向政府通报前沿模型的情况。</p>
  <p>这里没有一套连贯的政策。美国政府内部各派势力各自为政，朝相反方向使劲，Anthropic夹在中间，只能多方周旋——一家市值数百亿美元的公司，在规则如此混乱的时候，能做的也就是这样。</p>
  <p>Clark确认Anthropic未来也会向政府通报新模型。关系已经破裂，但对话没有停止。这大概是对当前美国AI治理最诚实的描述：破裂，但仍在沟通。</p>

<h2>The US Government Is Suing Anthropic and Begging to Use Its AI at the Same Time</h2>
<p>Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark walked onto the stage at the Semafor World Economy Summit on April 14, 2026, and confirmed something that sounds like a typo: his company is actively briefing the Trump administration about its new Mythos model, even though the Pentagon has labeled Anthropic a supply-chain risk and cut off all business ties.</p>
<p>Not a metaphor. Both things are happening at once.</p>
<p>Mythos, announced April 7, is Anthropic&#8217;s most capable model for coding and agentic tasks. That&#8217;s the company&#8217;s own description. The model is not available to the public. Anthropic says it&#8217;s limiting access because Mythos, despite not being trained for cybersecurity work, happens to be dangerously good at finding software vulnerabilities. Whether that&#8217;s genuine caution or a smart enterprise sales strategy depends on who you ask.</p>
<h2>What Actually Happened</h2>
<p>Before Mythos launched, Vice President JD Vance and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent summoned tech CEOs to discuss AI security and cyber threats. That meeting, reported by CNBC on April 10, set the stage for what came next.</p>
<p>After the launch, Bessent and Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell called bank executives in for another session. This time the message was different. According to Bloomberg, officials encouraged those banks to test Mythos for vulnerability detection. JPMorgan Chase was already listed as an early partner. Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, and Morgan Stanley are now reportedly testing it too.</p>
<p>Five of the largest banks in the United States, quietly urged by regulators to adopt a model from a company the government is simultaneously fighting in court.</p>
<h2>The Court Fight</h2>
<p>Anthropic sued the Department of Defense in March 2026 after the Pentagon designated the company a supply-chain risk. The designation followed failed negotiations over military access to Anthropic&#8217;s AI systems. Anthropic wanted limits on how its models could be used, specifically around mass surveillance of Americans and fully autonomous weapons. The Pentagon wanted unrestricted access. OpenAI ended up winning that contract instead.</p>
<p>A San Francisco federal judge later granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction blocking the Trump administration from enforcing a ban on Claude. But on April 8, an appeals court dealt Anthropic a loss, refusing to temporarily block the Pentagon&#8217;s blacklisting.</p>
<p>Clark downplayed the dispute at the Semafor event, calling it a &#8220;narrow contracting dispute&#8221; and saying Anthropic didn&#8217;t want it to interfere with national security engagement. His exact words: &#8220;Our position is the government has to know about this stuff, and we have to find new ways for the government to partner with a private sector that is making things that are truly revolutionizing the economy.&#8221;</p>
<h2>Why UK Regulators Are Paying Attention</h2>
<p>The Financial Times reported that UK financial regulators are also discussing risks posed by Mythos. The model&#8217;s ability to find zero-day vulnerabilities at scale has governments on edge, and not just in Washington. A tool that can audit code for exploits faster than any human team is either the best defensive weapon a bank could ask for or a serious offensive capability in the wrong hands.</p>
<p>Washington Post columnist wrote on April 12 that the Pentagon&#8217;s ban on Anthropic looks &#8220;shortsighted&#8221; given Mythos&#8217;s ability to detect critical software flaws. The headline practically wrote itself.</p>
<h2>The Employment Question Nobody Can Answer</h2>
<p>Clark also addressed AI&#8217;s impact on jobs at the summit. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has previously warned that AI advances could push unemployment to Depression-era levels. Clark, who leads a team of economists at Anthropic, was more measured. He said the company is currently seeing &#8220;some potential weakness in early graduate employment&#8221; across select industries. Not mass displacement yet, but early tremors.</p>
<p>That&#8217;s worth watching. When the company building the technology admits entry-level white-collar jobs are starting to soften, it&#8217;s probably not marketing talk.</p>
<h2>What Makes This Strange</h2>
<p>The whole situation is a contradiction that tells you something about where AI policy actually stands in 2026. The Department of Defense says Anthropic is too risky to do business with. The Treasury Department and the Fed are nudging banks to adopt Anthropic&#8217;s product. The appeals court says the Pentagon can blacklist Anthropic. A lower court says the government can&#8217;t ban Claude. The company is suing the government and briefing it on frontier models at the same time.</p>
<p>There is no coherent policy here. There are competing fiefdoms inside the US government pulling in opposite directions, and Anthropic is caught in the middle, playing every angle because that&#8217;s what a company worth tens of billions of dollars does when the rules are this muddled.</p>
<p>Clark confirmed Anthropic will brief the government on future models too. The relationship is broken but the conversations haven&#8217;t stopped. That&#8217;s probably the most honest description of US AI governance right now: broken, but still talking.</p>
