  <h2>Anthropic百亿美元押注AWS，AI竞赛变成电力竞赛</h2>
  <p><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute">Anthropic</a>在4月20日把一件事说得很明白：AI竞赛的下一阶段，靠的不是花哨演示或模型质量的空泛说辞，而是电力、芯片，以及大到让大型科技公司投资者都皱眉头的合同。该公司表示，未来10年将在Amazon Web Services上投入超过1000亿美元，而<a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-invests-additional-5-billion-anthropic-ai">Amazon</a>将立即投入50亿美元，若商业里程碑达成，还有最多200亿美元的追加空间。</p>
  <p>光是这个数字就足以成为大新闻。更值得注意的细节是附带的计算目标。Anthropic称，该协议锁定了最多5吉瓦的算力，用于训练和运行Claude。在云计算的语境里，这不是一笔常规采购，而是工业规模的电力主张，把话题从软件拉到了基础设施层面。</p>
  <h2>最关键的5吉瓦</h2>
  <p>Anthropic没有用含糊的语言描述这笔交易。该公司表示，已与Amazon签署新协议，锁定最多5吉瓦的算力，用于训练和部署Claude。其中一部分很快会到位。Anthropic说，新的Trainium2算力将在2026年上半年上线，到2026年底，Trainium2和Trainium3合计算力预计接近1吉瓦。</p>
  <p>这个时间点很重要，因为AI公司通常不会宣布长达十年的基础设施计划，除非现有需求已经让系统承压。Anthropic直接承认了这一点。该公司称，其年化收入已超过300亿美元，而2025年底约为90亿美元。它还表示，消费者增长损害了免费、Pro、Max和Team用户的可靠性和性能，尤其在高峰时段。在供应商通常只谈增长势头、把故障留给用户去抱怨的市场里，这种坦白很少见。</p>
  <p>这些数字说明，Claude不再被当作一个有前景的产品线。在Anthropic和Amazon内部，它已经被当作一个公用事业规模的负载来对待。</p>
  <h2>Amazon不是为面子写支票</h2><p>亚马逊在这笔交易中的角色容易被忽视，如果头条新闻只盯着Anthropic的话。该公司表示现在向Anthropic投资50亿美元，另有最多200亿美元与未来里程碑挂钩。加上亚马逊此前已投入的80亿美元，这层关系正在成为AI市场中最深的合作之一。</p>
  <p>这同时也是一桩芯片生意。Anthropic表示，这一承诺覆盖Graviton CPU以及从Trainium2到Trainium4的芯片，并有权在亚马逊未来推出新一代硅片时购买。亚马逊CEO安迪·贾西直言不讳：“我们的定制AI芯片为客户提供高性能、低成本，因此需求旺盛。”他补充说，Anthropic决定未来十年在AWS Trainium上运行大语言模型，“反映了我们在定制芯片上共同取得的进展。”</p>
  <p>这句话之所以重要，是因为亚马逊需要的不仅是云收入。它需要证明Trainium能吸引顶级AI实验室摆脱对Nvidia的依赖。如果Anthropic成为这项努力的旗舰客户，AWS得到的将比基准测试图表更有价值——它得到了一个活生生的案例研究。</p>
  <h2>Claude深入融入AWS</h2>
  <p>这次合作不限于算力。Anthropic表示，完整的Claude平台将直接在AWS内提供，私有测试版已开始。该公司用实际角度描述：同一个AWS账户、同样的控制、同样的账单，无需额外凭证或合同。对企业买家来说，这个细节几乎和模型本身一样重要。采购团队喜欢更少的供应商，安全团队喜欢熟悉的防护措施，财务团队喜欢一张账单。</p>
  <p>Anthropic还表示，已有超过10万客户在Amazon Bedrock上运行Claude。亚马逊重复了这一数字，并补充了旨在让这笔交易显得落地的案例研究。据亚马逊称，Lyft通过Bedrock上由Claude驱动的助手，将平均客服解决时间缩短了87%。辉瑞则用Claude搜索每个药物开发项目约2万份文档，每年为科学家节省1.6万小时，同时将基础设施成本削减55%。</p>
  <p>这些例子确实是精心打磨的企业证明点。但它们仍有作用。亚马逊在告诉客户，Anthropic不只是一个租用算力的实验室，而是一个可以围绕其组织更广泛AWS AI业务的主要租户。</p><h2>这笔交易折射出整个市场的某种尴尬</h2>
  <p>表面上看，Anthropic只是锁定了大量芯片和云容量。更深一层看，这对行业其他玩家并不光彩。前沿AI如今越来越像一门靠人才和模型设计吃饭的生意，但对电力、数据中心和定制芯片的掌控，比多数高管愿意公开承认的更重要。</p>
  <p>Anthropic还提到地理扩张，称新协议将扩大在亚洲和欧洲的推理能力。这个细节值得注意。需求不再集中于单一地区，云服务商正赶在延迟、监管或区域短缺成为更大问题之前，把容量部署到付费用户附近。</p>
  <p>还有一层变数。Anthropic表示，Claude仍是唯一同时运行在AWS、Google Cloud和Microsoft Azure上的前沿模型。纸面上，这让Anthropic保持灵活。实际上，这份亚马逊协议让AWS成为重心。一家公司不会在十年内承诺投入1000亿美元，除非它已经想清楚未来最可能在哪里落地。</p>
  <h2>Project Rainier只是预演，这次才是真金白银</h2>
  <p>两家公司都回溯了Project Rainier，那个双方共同搭建的巨型AI计算集群。亚马逊称其为全球最大集群之一，由近50万颗Trainium2芯片构成。Anthropic则表示，它已使用超过100万颗Trainium2芯片来训练和运行Claude。无论按芯片、吉瓦还是美元计算，传递的信息都一样：这不再是试验性联盟。</p>
  <p>Anthropic CEO兼联合创始人Dario Amodei把这次公告直接归因于用户需求。他说：“用户告诉我们，Claude在工作中越来越不可或缺，我们需要建设基础设施来跟上快速增长的需求。”这句话听起来克制，但也包含警告。如果需求迫使一家据报年化收入300亿美元的公司提前按这种规模购买电力，那么顶级AI算力的供应紧张远未结束。</p><p>这或许是本周最大AI新闻的真正要点。Anthropic和亚马逊不只是宣布了一项合作。他们宣告，前沿AI正在变成一种旧工业意义上的基础设施生意，涉及巨额投入、漫长的建设周期，以及对管道控制权的争夺。尽管外界热议智能体、副驾驶和模型个性，这个市场最终仍偏向那些能保证电力不断的人。</p>

<h2>Anthropic’s $100 Billion AWS Bet Turns the AI Race Into a Power Contest</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute">Anthropic</a> made one point painfully clear on April 20: the next stage of the AI fight will not be won with clever demos or vague talk about model quality. It will be won with electricity, chips, and contracts large enough to make even Big Tech investors blink. The company said it will spend more than $100 billion on Amazon Web Services over the next 10 years, while <a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-invests-additional-5-billion-anthropic-ai">Amazon</a> will put in $5 billion immediately and leave room for up to $20 billion more if commercial milestones are met.</p>
<p>That number alone would have made this a major story. The more revealing detail was the compute target attached to it. Anthropic said the agreement secures up to 5 gigawatts of capacity for training and running Claude. In cloud terms, that is not a routine procurement deal. It is an industrial-scale power claim, the kind of figure that moves the conversation from software to infrastructure.</p>
<h2>The part that matters most: 5 gigawatts</h2>
<p>Anthropic did not describe the deal in fuzzy language. The company said it has signed a new agreement with Amazon that will secure up to 5 gigawatts of capacity for training and deploying Claude. Some of that starts arriving soon. Anthropic said new Trainium2 capacity will come online in the first half of 2026, and nearly 1 gigawatt of combined Trainium2 and Trainium3 capacity is expected by the end of 2026.</p>
<p>That timeline matters because AI companies do not usually announce decade-long infrastructure plans unless current demand is already straining the system. Anthropic admitted exactly that. The company said its run-rate revenue has passed $30 billion, up from about $9 billion at the end of 2025. It also said consumer growth has hurt reliability and performance for free, Pro, Max, and Team users, especially at peak hours. That is a rare bit of candor in a market where providers usually talk about momentum and leave the outages for frustrated customers to document.</p>
<p>The numbers suggest Claude is no longer being treated as a promising product line. Inside Anthropic and Amazon, it is being treated as a utility-scale workload.</p>
<h2>Amazon is not writing a check for bragging rights</h2>
<p>Amazon’s side of the deal is easy to miss if the headline stays stuck on Anthropic. The company said it is investing $5 billion in Anthropic now, with up to another $20 billion tied to future milestones. Added to the $8 billion Amazon had already invested, the relationship is becoming one of the deepest pairings in the AI market.</p>
<p>This is also a chip story. Anthropic said the commitment spans Graviton CPUs and Trainium2 through Trainium4 chips, with the option to buy future generations of Amazon silicon as they appear. Andy Jassy, Amazon’s CEO, put the pitch in blunt terms: “Our custom AI silicon offers high performance at significantly lower cost for customers, which is why it’s in such hot demand.” He added that Anthropic’s decision to run large language models on AWS Trainium for the next decade “reflects the progress we’ve made together on custom silicon.”</p>
<p>That quote lands because Amazon needs more than cloud revenue here. It needs proof that Trainium can pull top-tier AI labs away from Nvidia-heavy habits. If Anthropic becomes the flagship customer for that effort, AWS gets something more valuable than a benchmark chart. It gets a living case study.</p>
<h2>Claude is moving deeper into AWS</h2>
<p>The partnership is not limited to raw capacity. Anthropic said the full Claude Platform will become available directly inside AWS, with private beta access already underway. The company framed it in practical terms: same AWS account, same controls, same billing, no extra credentials or contracts. For enterprise buyers, that detail matters almost as much as the model itself. Procurement teams like fewer vendors. Security teams like familiar guardrails. Finance teams like one bill.</p>
<p>Anthropic also said more than 100,000 customers already run Claude on Amazon Bedrock. Amazon repeated that figure and added case studies meant to make the deal feel operational. Lyft, according to Amazon, cut average customer service resolution time by 87% with a Claude-powered assistant on Bedrock. Pfizer, Amazon said, is using Claude to search roughly 20,000 documents per drug development project, saving scientists 16,000 hours a year while trimming infrastructure costs by 55%.</p>
<p>Those examples are polished corporate proof points, yes. Still, they serve a purpose. Amazon is trying to tell customers that Anthropic is not merely a lab renting compute. It is a major tenant around which a broader AWS AI business can be organized.</p>
<h2>This deal says something awkward about the whole market</h2>
<p>The obvious reading is that Anthropic just locked in a huge supply of chips and cloud capacity. The sharper reading is less flattering for the rest of the industry. Frontier AI now looks increasingly like a business where talent and model design matter, but access to power, data centers, and custom silicon matters more than most executives like to admit in public.</p>
<p>Anthropic also pointed to geographic expansion, saying the new agreement will expand inference in Asia and Europe. That detail matters. Demand is no longer concentrated in one region, and cloud providers are racing to place capacity close to paying users before latency, regulation, or regional shortages become a bigger problem.</p>
<p>There is another wrinkle. Anthropic said Claude remains the only frontier model available across AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure. On paper, that keeps Anthropic flexible. In reality, this Amazon agreement makes AWS the center of gravity. A company does not commit $100 billion over a decade unless it has already decided where its future is most likely to be built.</p>
<h2>Project Rainier was the preview. This is the real commitment</h2>
<p>Both companies pointed back to Project Rainier, the giant AI compute cluster they launched together. Amazon described it as one of the world’s largest clusters, built with nearly half a million Trainium2 chips. Anthropic said it already uses more than one million Trainium2 chips to train and serve Claude. Whether one counts chips, gigawatts, or dollars, the message is the same: this is no longer an experimental alliance.</p>
<p>Dario Amodei, Anthropic’s CEO and co-founder, tied the announcement directly to user demand. “Our users tell us Claude is increasingly essential to how they work, and we need to build the infrastructure to keep pace with rapidly growing demand,” he said. That statement sounds measured. It also contains a warning. If demand is forcing a company with a reported $30 billion run rate to pre-buy power on this scale, the supply crunch around top-tier AI compute is nowhere close to over.</p>
<p>That may be the real takeaway from this week’s biggest AI story. Anthropic and Amazon did not just announce a partnership. They announced that frontier AI is becoming an infrastructure business in the old industrial sense, with giant commitments, long lead times, and an obsession with who controls the pipes. For all the chatter about agents, copilots, and model personalities, this market still bends toward whoever can keep the lights on.</p>
